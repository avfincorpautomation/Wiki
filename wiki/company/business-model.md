# Business model

## What the company reports

- **What it makes:** structural steel tubes and hollow sections (CHS/SHS/RHS), GP pipes, galvanised and pre-galvanised products, "Steel for Green" building products (door frames, planks, fencing), heavy structural tubes up to 1,000 mm × 1,000 mm, and coated/roofing products. More than 5,000 SKUs; size range 10 mm × 10 mm to 1,000 mm × 1,000 mm, thickness 0.5–40 mm ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.35–36).
- **Raw material:** hot-rolled coil ([[hrc]]) bought from primary steel mills and formed into ERW tubes, using [[direct-forming-technology]] at most plants (p.164, p.362). FY26 standalone HRC purchases were ₹9,004.26 cr / 18,02,264 ton, with no commodity hedging (p.180).
- **Manufacturing:** 11 plants, ~5 Mn ton capacity at 31 March 2026; average utilisation ~70%, peak 95%+ (p.32, p.66, p.148). Locations: Sikandrabad (UP), Hyderabad, Bangalore, Hosur, Malur, Murbad, Raipur (incl. [[apl-apollo-building-products]]) and Umm Al Quwain, UAE ([[apl-apollo-tubes-company-llc]]) (p.68, p.136, p.148).
- **Plant specialisation:** Raipur is the hub for value-added and [[roofing-products]]; Dubai is export-focused; Sikandrabad serves high-volume standard product in North India (p.68).
- **Brands:** "APL Apollo" (premium) and "SG Premium" (value segment, competing with [[secondary-steel-patra]] pipes and regional makers). Management calls this a dual-brand strategy (p.13–14, p.148). *Superseded margin figure:* in August 2026 SG Premium EBITDA was restated to ₹0–1,000/ton ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.13); see [[sg-premium]] and C23 in [[open-questions]]. See [[sg-premium]]. In January 2026 management said the APL Apollo brand premium of ₹3,000–4,000/ton "is now the new normal", and that SG Premium earns ₹1,500–2,000/ton EBITDA ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.3, p.7).
- **Freight and plant localisation:** each plant now serves its local market and complex products are concentrated at Raipur. Freight from Raipur is ~₹2,600/ton against ~₹1,150 from local plants, with a local target of ₹750 ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.7).
- **Distribution:** 800+ distributors/dealers, 50,000+ retailers, 2,00,000+ fabricators, 300+ towns/cities (p.101, p.148). Sales to dealers/distributors are ~91% of sales; top-10 dealers take 44% of dealer sales (p.210). Institutional sales go directly to PEB makers and EPC contractors (p.103).
- **Pricing:** management says it runs a full [[pass-through-pricing]] model: raw material price changes reach the channel in 5–8 days; dealers turn inventory ~15 times a year (p.43, p.101).
- **End-use mix FY26:** housing 67%, commercial buildings 19%, infrastructure 10%, industrial & agriculture 4% (p.152).
- **Profit metric used by management:** [[ebitda-per-ton]] — ₹5,161 in FY26 (p.25).

> [!warning] Contradiction
> Dealer count is "800+" on p.101, p.148 and p.210, but p.32 shows "2,000+" next to "800+" under Distribution. If 2,000+ means towns/cities, it conflicts with "300+ towns and cities" on p.101. See [[open-questions]].
- **August 2026 additions** ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]]):
  - SG Premium is priced at ~₹58,000/ton, 6–7% below the APL Apollo brand (p.13).
  - Pre-engineered buildings use ~20% steel tube by weight, and management claims 65–70% share with the top PEB companies (p.12).
  - Solar-structure products are expected to reach 4–5% of volume in 2–3 years (p.13). The deck sizes the solar opportunity at ~830,000 ton a year by 2030 ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.24).
  - The Mumbai plant is to become export-only, with a new 0.5 Mn ton plant on the Pune corridor cutting freight to the Pune market from ₹1,100–1,200 to ₹200–300/ton (p.14–15).
- **October 2026 additions** ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-concall]]):
  - Roofing capacity is 50,000–60,000 ton a month (~7 lakh ton a year), for 0.4–3.5 mm sheets. End use is 50% housing, 25% commercial/industrial and 25% other (11:14–12:44).
  - A new specialised mill (up to 250×250 mm, 16 mm) is sampling products for defence and aerospace. Items have been developed for ISRO and DRDO (01:16, 37:24).
  - The APL Apollo brand now includes light structures, 25% of its Q2 mix (22:29).
  - The CMD claims black-pipe pricing of ~₹70,000/ton against ≤₹68,000 for peers (26:25; reconstructed figure).

## Our read

- This is a converter's spread business. Raw material (materials + traded goods + inventory change) was ₹19,509 cr, about 85% of ₹23,079 cr revenue in FY26 (our calculation, p.344). Earnings depend on holding the spread over HRC.
- The brand claims (pricing power, "price setter") are management's. The hard evidence in this source is EBITDA/ton rising from ₹3,797 to ₹5,161 while volume grew 11% (p.25).
- Dealers are 91% of sales, and the top 10 take 44% of dealer sales ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.210). That concentration is the other side of the near-zero working capital story. See [[risks]].
- The Q1 FY27 call shows the cost of the dual-brand model when the Patra gap widens. SG Premium volume fell 35% QoQ, and management says it will not chase it at a ₹10–12/kg gap ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.14). See [[sg-premium]].
