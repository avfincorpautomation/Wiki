## Our read

- **Derived numbers (our calculations from p.344 and p.24):**
  - Gross spread (revenue − materials − traded goods − inventory change): ₹3,570 cr, ~₹10,226/ton (FY25: ₹2,819 cr, ~₹8,928/ton).
  - Realisation: ~₹66,110/ton (FY25: ~₹65,515/ton).
  - PAT per ton: ~₹3,446 (FY25: ~₹2,397).
  - Effective tax rate: 22.7% (FY25: 21.2%).
- Of the ~₹1,000 cr jump in net cash in Q4, profit explains only ~₹350 cr and inventory release ~₹250 cr. "Better payment terms from the creditors" probably covers much of the rest. Q4 is therefore when the [[supplier-finance]] payables (₹1,382 cr at year-end) did much of the work. The FY26 "net cash" is partly funded by suppliers' banks (our inference).
- The traded-goods regrouping (₹619 cr) inflates revenue but not tonnage, so revenue per ton is not comparable across quarters. Use EBITDA/ton instead.
- Realisation per ton barely moved while gross spread per ton rose ~15%. The margin gain came from the spread over HRC, not from higher selling prices overall.
- **Q1 FY27 cash went backwards.** Operating cash flow was negative (−₹30 cr) in a quarter with ₹411 cr of EBITDA. The main cause was a ₹456 cr inventory build while volume fell 19% QoQ. Payables rose ₹256 cr and partly offset it, so suppliers again funded part of the working capital. Capex of ₹216 cr in one quarter is above the ₹500–600 cr a year the CMD gave in May ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.6), if that rate is sustained (our calculation). Interest rose 23% QoQ despite slightly lower debt, which again points to bill-discounting or supplier-finance costs (see [[supplier-finance]]).
- **Q2 FY27: borrowing while holding cash.** Gross debt doubled to ≈₹918 cr in H1 while cash, bank balances and FDs rose to ~₹2,700 cr (our sum from Q2R p.10). Q2 finance cost of ₹52 cr is ~30% annualised on that debt, which points to financing costs beyond plain borrowings: bill discounting, supplier finance, or acceptances reclassified as debt (our inference). Reported "net cash" went up while the liability side grew. Reading the balance sheet gross is more informative than the net-cash headline.
- Finance costs of ₹125 cr look high for a self-described net-cash company. Interest on ₹452 cr of borrowings would not explain it alone. Management has now said bill-discounting charges are part of it ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.13), but has given no split. See [[open-questions]].
- **Net cash depends on the definition.** Excluding FDs, net cash fell by roughly half in H1 FY27 (₹434 cr → ₹232 cr on filed figures; our calc.). Including them, it rose. Both are arithmetically right. The question is how freely the FDs can be used (open question 34).
