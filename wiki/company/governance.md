### Audit

- Statutory auditor: Walker Chandiok & Co LLP, appointed at the 15 September 2025 AGM for five years. The predecessor was Deloitte Haskins & Sells LLP, which audited FY25 (p.155, p.337). See [[walker-chandiok]].
- Unmodified opinion; no fraud reported (p.155).
- **Key audit matter:** ₹603.13 cr of PPE additions, mainly tube mills bought from specific vendors without multiple quotations because management treats them as proprietary and customised (p.334–335).
- Audit trail exception: the audit trail feature "was not enabled at the database level" for direct data changes in the accounting software (p.339; same remark in standalone report, p.255).
- The UAE subsidiary was audited by another auditor; two Indian subsidiaries were jointly audited with VAPS & Company (p.337, p.180).
- Total fees to the statutory auditor (parent): ₹1.04 cr audit + ₹0.01 cr other services (p.180).

## Our read

- Promoter holding of 28% (28.26% at FY26, [[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.375; 28.2% at Sep 2026, [[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results-note]], p.2) is low for an Indian promoter-run company, so foreign institutions are the swing holders.
- The single-vendor capex KAM matters because the company is about to spend heavily on capacity. Watch who the tube-mill vendors are and whether any are related.
- The tripling of logistics spend with a promoter-linked company, and the new ₹10 cr payment to a promoter sports entity, are small against revenue but go in the wrong direction.
- An auditor change in the same year as record profits is not a red flag by itself (it may be rotation), but the reason is not given in what I read.
- **New related-party channel (August 2026):** the shared-services company puts APL Apollo's HR, IT and branding costs into a vehicle it will own only ≤20% of, shared with other promoter-group companies. The equity is trivial; the fees are what matter. Track them in FY27 related-party disclosures, alongside [[sg-green-logistics]].
- **Less disclosure:** dropping the product-line margin table removes the main tool for checking management's margin explanations. It happened in the same quarter that two of those explanations were restated (C22 and C23 in [[open-questions]]).
- **Q2 FY27:** the SSC went from a ≤20% associate (August) to a planned 51% subsidiary (October), with the extra shares bought from promoter entities. Once it is a subsidiary, its costs are consolidated. But the services it bills to other promoter companies (SG Mart, SG Finserv, Apollo Pipes) become related-party revenue of an APL Apollo subsidiary. The structure still needs watching.
- Two disposals (Blue Ocean, A-25 land) went to buyers who are not named. The amounts are small, but naming the buyers would rule out related parties.
