# Pass-through pricing

**What it is:** a pricing model where changes in raw material (HRC) prices are passed on to customers quickly, so the converter's spread per ton stays roughly stable.

## What the company reports

- Management calls its model "a complete pass-through pricing model … input cost movements never reach the margin line. Steel prices move. Margins do not." ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.43).
- Raw material price movements are "reflected across the channel within 5 to 8 days", and dealers turn inventory about 15 times a year (p.101).
- Management also says it raised prices in January 2025 and the increase held through FY26 (p.11, p.19, p.51).
- The company does not hedge HRC with derivatives (p.180).
- On the Q3 FY26 call: "HRC is a pass-through for us … there is no assumption on the HR coil pricing" (CSO). The exception is a move of "10% or more in a single quarter … which happens like once in 10 years". Management says the whole industry, not just APL Apollo, now uses the 5–8 day pass-through ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.13, p.16–17). The new steel safeguard duty from December 2025 raised raw material prices but had "no impact" because of pass-through (p.15).

- On the Q4 FY26 call (May 2026): HRC rose ~₹4,500/ton on 1 April and ~₹3,000/ton more from April to May, and "that much price hike we took". Management said inventory gains were "very, very minuscule" because inventory churns in under 30 days and Indian mills revise prices monthly ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.6, p.16).
- Q1 FY27: "If steel prices were going up by INR1,000 per ton, we tried to improve our prices for our product by plus 100 plus INR200 per ton over and above steel price increase", lifting gross profit ~₹1,000/ton QoQ ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.4). The CMD says prices rose "almost INR8,000 to INR7,000" in Q4, and dealers destocked in expectation of a fall (p.8). From July some prices were cut on 15–20% of the basket (p.16).

## Our read

- Q2 FY25 (EBITDA/ton ₹1,821) shows what a sharp HRC move does ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-presentation]], p.7). Management calls that a once-a-decade event.
- The claim that "margins do not" move is contradicted by the company's own history: EBITDA/ton ranged from ₹3,797 to ₹5,386 over FY22–26 (p.25). Pass-through protects the spread over weeks, not the level of the spread across cycles.
- With inventory at ~23 days (p.39) and no hedging, a sharp HRC fall still causes inventory losses.
- Q1 FY27 shows the trade-off clearly: pricing above cost protected EBITDA/ton but helped cause channel destocking and a 19% QoQ volume drop ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.7).
