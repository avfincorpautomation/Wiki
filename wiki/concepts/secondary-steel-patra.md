# Secondary steel / "Patra" pipes

**What it is:** tubes made by smaller, often unorganised producers from secondary steel (scrap or sponge-iron based) strip, known locally as "Patra". They are cheaper and of lower, less consistent quality than tubes made from primary HRC.

## What the company reports

- The MD&A cites industry estimates of ~9 Mn ton of structural tube volume in FY24, of which ~3.6 Mn ton was secondary/Patra-based. It projects 17.3 Mn ton by 2030, with Patra growing only to ~4.0 Mn ton and HRC-based tubes from 4.1 to 13.3 Mn ton ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.144).
- Secondary tubes are said to hold ~45–50% share, because they are cheaper (p.145).
- The Q3 FY26 presentation chart shows the FY24 ~9 Mn ton market split 4.5 HRC-based / 4.5 scrap-based, rising to 13.3 / 4.0 Mn ton by 2030 ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-presentation]], p.23). That FY24 split differs again from the annual report's 4.1 / 3.6.
- The Q4 FY26 presentation rebases the market to FY26: ~10 Mn ton total, split 5.0 HRC-based / 5.0 sponge-iron-based, rising to 18.3 Mn ton by FY31E (13.3 HRC-based) ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-presentation]], p.23).
- The [[sg-premium]] brand was launched to compete in this value segment without diluting the APL Apollo brand. The MD&A describes it as "aimed at cannibalising the patra segment" (p.13, p.149).
- BIS quality norms and GST formalisation are cited as pushing demand towards organised players (p.49–50, p.142).

> [!warning] Contradiction
> Within the same report, the secondary/Patra share is given as ~45–50% (p.145), while the 3.6 of ~9 Mn ton FY24 figures (p.144) imply ~40%, and the organised sector is said to be ~65% of the market (p.142). The sources and definitions differ. See [[open-questions]].
- August 2026: the primary–secondary steel price gap is ₹10–12/kg, against ₹3–5/kg in Q4 FY26. The CMD says this "has a big impact on our 30% business" and may last "another one, two, or three quarters". He expects new blast-furnace HRC capacity to close the gap eventually ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.6–7, p.14).
- October 2026: the CMD expects to take "10–15–20% share" of the secondary-steel segment over one to two years. He says the company is "in a comfortable position" when the gap between primary and secondary steel widens ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-concall]], 48:36).
> [!warning] Contradiction
> The unofficial Q2 transcript has the CMD aiming to *take* 10–20% of the secondary-steel segment ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-concall]], 48:36). The results note and Axis report a plan to *reduce* the lower-margin business to ~10–20% of the mix over 1–2 years ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results-note]], p.7; [[src-2026-10-axis-q2-fy27-note]], p.1). See C32 in [[open-questions]].

## Our read

If the FY26 market is ~10 Mn ton, half of it HRC-based (Q4P p.23), then APL Apollo's 3.49 Mn ton (including ~0.3 Mn ton from Dubai) is roughly 65–70% of the HRC-based segment and ~33% of the whole market (our calculation). The "65% market share" claims are therefore probably share of the HRC-based or organised segment, not of all structural tubes.

The size of the Patra pool is the main share-gain runway claimed for SG Premium. Independent industry data is needed.
- The SG Premium volume collapse in Q1 FY27 is the first hard test of the Patra-displacement thesis, and the gap moved against it. Track the gap each quarter.
