# SG Premium (value brand)

**What it is:** APL Apollo's second brand. It sells base-category structural tubes at the lowest price point, to compete with regional makers and sponge-iron/[[secondary-steel-patra]] pipes without discounting the flagship APL Apollo brand.

## What the company reports

- The annual report calls it a "flanker brand" for the value segment, launched to cannibalise the Patra segment and use spare capacity ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.13–14, p.101, p.149).
- Management describes the two brands as "H1" (highest price) and "L1" (lowest price) in the market ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.5, p.10; [[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-presentation]], p.13).
- **Economics (management, Jan 2026):**
  - EBITDA ₹1,500–2,000/ton, against ~₹7,000 targeted for the APL Apollo brand (concall p.7).
  - Volume 60,000–70,000 ton in Q3 FY26, possibly up to 1 lakh ton a quarter (concall p.16). The CMD also cited ~50,000 ton a month (concall p.7). The two figures do not match.
  - Expected to stay "under 10%" of volume (concall p.18).
- Q4 FY26: SG Premium was "between 8% to 9%" of total volume ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.9), i.e. ~74,000–83,000 ton on 925 thousand ton (our calculation). That is closer to the 60–70k-a-quarter figure than to ~50k a month.
- On the same call the CMD said the company's volume "attached to" the Patra segment had been reduced to "less than 30%" (p.5). The base of that percentage is unclear.
- SG Premium volume is reported inside the "General" product line, which earned ₹3,149/ton EBITDA in Q3 FY26 on 392 thousand ton (presentation p.10).
- The aggressive stance: "We will sell SG premium no matter what the volume is, whatever the price. Maybe price is less than HR coil, or maybe price is less than patra" (concall p.18).
- Management says it was trialled for two to three months and pushed from October–November 2025, which coincided with monthly volume rising from 2.5 lakh to 3.7 lakh ton (concall p.5).
- **Reported volume (from August 2026):** FY26 230,862 ton; Q1 FY26 14,141, Q2 38,356, Q3 87,860, Q4 90,504; **Q1 FY27 58,686** ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.12). FY26 is 6.6% of volume and Q4 FY26 9.8% (our calculation). This settles C15: the ~50,000 ton a month figure was wrong, and the 60–70k-a-quarter figure was low.
- **Economics (August 2026):** price ~₹58,000/ton, 6–7% below the APL Apollo brand. EBITDA "range from INR0 to INR1,000 per ton depending on market opportunity" ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.13), and "around INR500 per ton" (p.17).
- The CMD says the strategy depends on the primary–secondary steel gap: at ₹10–12/kg (now) "there is no benefit in selling goods by losing cash"; at ₹3–5/kg (as in Q4 FY26) "this becomes a masterstroke", with EBITDA of ₹1,500–3,000 possible (p.14).

> [!warning] Contradiction
> SG Premium EBITDA was ₹1,500–2,000/ton in January ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.7). In August it is ₹0–1,000 or ~₹500 ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.13, p.17). See C23 in [[open-questions]].
- **Q2 FY27:** volume 26,910 ton (Q1 58,686; Q2 FY26 38,356), −54% QoQ, now 2.8% of volume ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-presentation]], p.13; our calc.). The CMD: "In SG Premium, I can't commit anything because we don't have visibility" ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-concall]], 24:34). The CSO says SG Premium volume "will start going up" if steel prices fall (43:55).

## Our read

- SG Premium appears to be the main reason volume jumped in Q3 FY26. It is a low-margin volume lever that also spreads fixed costs.
- "Price less than HR coil" means potentially selling below raw material cost. That could dilute EBITDA/ton and draw a competitive response.
- The two volume figures (60–70k a quarter vs ~50k a month) differ by a factor of about two. See [[open-questions]].
- **Q1 FY27:** the brand's volume fell 35% QoQ (90,504 → 58,686 ton; [[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.12) when the Patra gap widened, and management chose not to chase it. That is more disciplined than January's "whatever the price". It also shows that the Q3–Q4 FY26 volume jump depended on a narrow Patra gap that has since reversed.
