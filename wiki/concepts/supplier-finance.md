# Supplier finance (reverse factoring)

**What it is:** a bank pays the company's supplier early, and the company pays the bank later on agreed terms. The company still reports the amount as a trade payable, not as borrowings. In economic terms it can be bank funding that sits outside reported debt. From FY26 (1 April 2025), amendments to Ind AS 7 and Ind AS 107 require companies to disclose these arrangements.

## What the company reports

- The group has a supplier finance arrangement in which "participating suppliers have the option to receive early settlement of their invoices from banks"; the group "subsequently remits the payment to the banks in accordance with agreed terms" ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.386).
- Carrying amount in trade payables at 31 March 2026: **₹1,381.96 cr**, all of which suppliers had already received from the finance provider. Standalone: ₹982.97 cr (p.301, p.386).
- Payment due dates: 15–90 days after invoice for liabilities in the arrangement, vs 0–90 days for comparable payables outside it (p.386).
- Management says the arrangement "does not materially alter the credit period" and costs the group nothing extra. Comparatives were not given, using transitional relief (p.386).
- Total trade payables: ₹2,368.30 cr (p.343).

- On the Q4 FY26 call, management attributed part of the ~₹1,000 cr Q4 increase in net cash to "better payment terms from the creditors" ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.8). It did not quantify this or link it to the supplier finance arrangement.
- Q1 FY27: trade payables rose from ₹23,683 Mn to ₹26,240 Mn (+₹2,557 Mn) while inventory rose ₹4,562 Mn ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.20). The supplier-finance share at 30 June is not disclosed in quarterly filings. Interest cost rose 23% QoQ to ₹389 Mn although debt fell slightly (p.19).
- Q2 FY27: gross borrowings rose to ≈₹918 cr (31 Mar ₹452 cr), with net current borrowings of +₹538 cr in H1. Finance cost was ₹52.40 cr in the quarter ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results]], p.9–11). Trade payables were ₹25,653 Mn (31 Mar ₹23,683 Mn) ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-presentation]], p.20).
- [[icra]], February 2026: short-term non-fund-based limits (typically LCs and acceptances) fell from ₹1,000 cr to nil, while fund-based cash credit limits rose from ₹1,460 cr to ₹2,975 cr ([[src-2026-02-icra-rating]], p.1).

## Our read

- If supplier finance is treated as debt, gross debt rises from ₹452 cr to ~₹1,834 cr, and the company's net cash of ₹1,530 cr falls to roughly ₹150 cr (our calculation). The "net cash" and "zero working capital" claims are sensitive to this.
- Without the supplier finance payables, payables would be ~₹986 cr and net working capital (inventory + receivables − payables) would be about +₹810 cr, ~13 days of revenue, instead of about −9 days (our calculation, p.343).
- It is not yet clear whether the ₹125 cr FY26 finance cost ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.344) includes any cost of this arrangement. See [[open-questions]].
- The rise in interest with lower debt fits with bill-discounting or supplier-finance charges sitting in finance costs (our inference). The "₹500 cr liability" has still not been identified (see [[open-questions]]).
- One possibility is that part of the supplier-finance or acceptance payables was refinanced into bank borrowings (our inference, not confirmed). That would fit the ₹500 cr "liability" being "retired" by moving it, rather than paid off. The H2 FY27 balance sheet will tell.
- The ICRA limit shift came *before* the H1 FY27 jump in current borrowings (+₹538 cr). That strengthens the reading that acceptance or LC-type liabilities were refinanced into bank borrowings rather than repaid (still an inference). It also fits the "₹500 cr liability" being retired in name while debt rose.
