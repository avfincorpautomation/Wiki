## What the company does

APL Apollo describes itself as India's largest maker of structural steel tubes (hollow sections, GP pipes, galvanised, coated and heavy structural tubes, and steel building products), with 11 plants and ~5 Mn ton of capacity, including one plant in the UAE ([[src-2026-08-annual-report-fy26]], p.29, p.32, p.148). It sells mainly through 800+ distributors, under two brands: APL Apollo (premium) and SG Premium (value) (p.101, p.148). See [[business-model]].

## How it makes money

It buys hot-rolled coil ([[hrc]]) from steel mills and earns a conversion spread by forming it into tubes. Management tracks this as [[ebitda-per-ton]]. Higher-margin [[value-added-products]] were ~58% of FY26 sales (p.148). Management says price changes are passed through to dealers within 5–8 days ([[pass-through-pricing]], p.101). Housing is 67% of end use (p.152). There is one reportable segment; exports were ~9% of contract revenue (p.362, p.388). See [[segments]].

## What matters now

- **Outside views (Feb–Oct 2026):**
  - [[icra]] rates the company AA+ (Stable) / A1+, but expects only ~6.8 MTPA by FY28 and EBITDA/ton of ₹4,500–5,000. Its downgrade trigger is debt/OPBITDA above 0.75× ([[src-2026-02-icra-rating]], p.1, p.3).
  - Axis and Systematix rate it BUY, with targets of ₹2,500 and ₹2,517 ([[src-2026-10-axis-q2-fy27-note]], p.1; [[src-2026-10-systematix-q2-fy27-note]], p.1).
  - The share price was ₹2,078–2,214 on 26–27 October 2026, about 47× TTM earnings ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results-note]], p.2). See [[valuation]].
  - On a strict basis excluding FDs, Systematix puts net cash at only ₹2.3 Bn, against the company's ₹16.3 Bn ([[src-2026-10-systematix-q2-fy27-note]], p.1).

- **Q2 FY27: record margin, but volume guidance withdrawn and debt doubled.**
  - EBITDA/ton ₹5,999; management now calls ₹6,000 "sustainable" and says guidance will be revised after two such quarters.
  - Company-wide FY27 volume guidance was in effect dropped: only the APL Apollo brand and Roofing are committed ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-concall]], 06:31).
  - Gross profit per ton actually fell ~₹500 QoQ, so the margin gain is operating leverage (21:45).
  - Gross debt doubled and finance cost rose 35% QoQ, against the "liability-free" and "almost zero interest" promises ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results]], p.9–11).
  - The presentation overstates revenue growth and operating cash flow against the filing (C28–C29 in [[open-questions]]).
  - Restructuring done: Blue Ocean sold (₹160 cr; buyer unnamed) and APL Apollo Mart liquidated (₹109 cr). The shared-services company is to become a 51% subsidiary, with the extra shares bought from promoter entities ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results]], p.1–4, p.12, p.18).
- **Q1 FY27 missed the volume target but held margin.** Volume of 7.45 lakh ton came in against the ~8.75 lakh the CMD pointed to in May; interest cost rose instead of going to "almost zero"; EBITDA/ton held at ₹5,522. FY27 guidance (volume 15–20%, EBITDA 20%+) was kept, but it now depends on a back-ended path of Q2 10, Q3 10.5 and Q4 12 lakh ton ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.6, p.18; [[guidance-tracker]]).
- **Less visibility:** product-line EBITDA/ton is no longer disclosed. In the same quarter, management restated the January 2025 price increase (₹1,500 → ₹500/ton) and the SG Premium margin (₹1,500–2,000 → ₹0–1,000/ton) ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.13, p.17). See [[open-questions]] C22–C23.
- **New governance items:** a promoter-group [[group-shared-services-company]], and the closure of [[apollo-metalex]]'s A-25 unit with a land sale ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-results]], p.1–2).
- **Delivering FY27 guidance:** volume +15–20%, EBITDA +20–25%, PAT +25–30% (p.22). This is lower than the January 2026 guidance (20%, at least 4.2 Mn ton, at least ₹5,500/ton), and the January targets for Q4 FY26 and FY26 were missed ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-concall]], p.5–11). See [[guidance-tracker]].
- **Where the margin gain came from:** EBITDA/ton rose in every product line, most in the "General" line (₹1,705 → ₹3,149/ton, FY25 → Q3 FY26), which points to brand pricing rather than mix ([[src-2026-01-q3-fy26-presentation]], p.10–11; [[kpis]]). The new [[sg-premium]] value brand earns only ₹1,500–2,000/ton by January's figure; in August management put it at ₹0–1,000/ton.
- **What happened after February 2026:** management says the Middle East war (steel and gas shortages, Dubai at 40%, destocking) derailed Q4. April 2026 volume was only 2.5 lakh ton. The CMD now says 20% growth in FY27 "is difficult" and that the company is putting margin before volume ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.3–8, p.11).
- **Capacity build-out:** 5 → 8 Mn ton by FY28 (ICRA expects ~6.8 MTPA; [[src-2026-02-icra-rating]], p.1), then 10 Mn ton by 2030, said to be self-funded (p.5, p.20). The May 2026 call's "2 and 2.5 years" for the remaining capex points to FY29 ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.6). In August the CSO said "two and a half years", while the deck kept "By FY28" ([[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-concall]], p.4; [[src-2026-08-q1-fy27-presentation]], p.10). See [[capital-allocation]].
- **Shareholder returns:** a dividend increase or buyback is flagged once the ₹500 cr liability is cleared in Q1–Q2 FY27 ([[src-2026-05-q4-fy26-concall]], p.7). There was no visible progress in Q1 (debt down only ~₹23 cr; net cash down ₹126 cr).
- **Can EBITDA/ton hold at ₹5,000+?** The target is ₹5,300–5,700 (p.20), against FY25's ₹3,797.
- **How "net cash" the balance sheet really is**, given ₹1,382 cr of [[supplier-finance]] payables (p.386). At 30 September 2026 gross debt had also doubled to ≈₹918 cr ([[src-2026-10-q2-fy27-results]], p.10).
- **Governance items:** single-vendor capex KAM, the auditor change, and rising promoter-linked related-party spend. See [[governance]].
